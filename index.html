The Problem

Disposable vapes don't disappear when you're finished with them.

Disposable vapes contain batteries and electronic components that can become electronic waste when thrown away. Re-Charge is exploring whether these materials can be recovered and safely put to better use.

To be clear: this isn't about blaming anyone who vapes. It's about a genuinely good piece of technology — a rechargeable lithium battery — locked inside a product designed to be used once. The design is the problem. The people returning their devices are the solution.

And here's the part that drives Re-Charge: the battery inside a "disposable" vape is a rechargeable cell, built to survive hundreds of charge cycles5 — discarded not because it's broken, but because the product around it is finished. The Re-Charge Concept

From disposable to reusable.

01

Collect

Old disposable vapes are collected through dedicated Re-Charge collection points.

02

Recover

The batteries and useful components are separated from the rest of the device.

03

Test

Recovered batteries are inspected and tested to determine whether they are suitable for safe reuse.

04

Re-Charge

Suitable cells could be rebuilt into products like portable power banks — housed in enclosures 3D-printed from recycled plastic bottles, so the whole product is made from waste.

Re-Charge is currently exploring the technical, safety, regulatory and economic feasibility of this concept.

Honest Answers

Questions we keep getting asked.

Is reusing recovered lithium batteries safe?

Only if it's done properly — and that's exactly what we're investigating. In the concept, every recovered cell would be inspected for damage, capacity-tested and safety-checked before it could ever be reused. Anything that doesn't pass would go to certified battery recycling instead of a bin. If we can't make that process reliably safe, Re-Charge won't happen.

Isn't vaping the real problem?

That's a debate for other people. Re-Charge doesn't promote or sell vaping in any way — and we don't judge anyone who vapes either. Vapers who return their devices are the heroes of this whole idea. Our focus is narrower: disposable vapes exist, they contain excellent rechargeable technology, and today that technology gets thrown away with nowhere better to go. We're exploring the "somewhere better".

Can I buy the Re-Charge One power bank?

Not yet — and maybe never. The Re-Charge One is a concept product. Whether it gets built depends on the technical, safety, regulatory and economic feasibility we're researching now, and on whether enough people say they want it. That's what the feedback form is for.

What would the power bank actually be made of?

As much waste as possible. The battery cells would be lithium-ion cells reclaimed from disposable vapes and individually tested for safe reuse. The enclosure would be 3D-printed from recycled plastic bottles. The goal is a product where almost nothing starts life as new material — waste in, useful product out.

What happens to the parts that can't be reused?

The concept is a circular one: components that aren't suitable for a second life would be routed to certified e-waste and battery recyclers rather than thrown away.

If something went wrong with a reclaimed battery, who's responsible?

We would be. If Re-Charge ever sells a product, we're responsible for its safety — reclaimed cells or not, that's non-negotiable. That's exactly why the concept includes individual cell testing, independent safety certification before anything is sold, clear labelling so every buyer knows the cells are reclaimed, and product-liability cover. You'll always be told what you're buying — but telling you is not a substitute for making it safe, and we won't sell anything we haven't proven.

Who is behind Re-Charge?

A small early-stage team exploring whether this idea deserves to exist. We're not a recycling corporation and we're not affiliated with any vape brand — right now we're just asking the question, in public, before building anything.
